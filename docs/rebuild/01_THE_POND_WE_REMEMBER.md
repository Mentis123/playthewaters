# 01 — The pond we remember

*Rebuild memo, 24 Oct 2026. Why the Ocarina of Time fishing pond swallowed days of people's lives, what it actually did, and how Reel Mobile's rebuild gets there with the 3D kit in `adamrappaport/public/map-assets` and the mobile discipline in `Mentis123/birb`. Sources at the end. Anything marked (unverified) is reported, not checked.*

> The owner's brief: *"we're still nailing the point on the discovery and the hunt and the trial and error and unknown and persistence."* This memo is organised around those five words.

---

## 1. What the pond actually was

The facts, because the rebuild has to be built from what the game did, not from the memory of it.

- **One room, one fee, one keeper.** 20 Rupees for unlimited time. You may keep one fish. There is one NPC, one way in, and nothing outside the pond can reach you. Nintendo Life called it *"an oasis of untouchable and unchanging calm."*
- **Controls were a hand, not a menu.** Z-target a fish, B casts, A brakes the line mid-flight, the stick wiggles the lure and angles the rod, A reels (A+R fast). The Rumble Pak was the bite. Tension was steered by holding the stick left/right against the fish. Too much and it's gone.
- **The water was readable.** Fish sizes were judged from their shadows. The largest fish held near the log and the reeds. Small fish scared big ones. Sunrise brought a feeding frenzy (Sun's Song set the time). Spooked fish fled to the deepest part. *"They'll dart away when I get too close, try their best to ignore me at the deepest part of the pond."*
- **Nothing was explained.** No tutorial. Goomba Stomp: fish behaviour had *"no rhyme or reason"* you must observe. Everything below was discovered by rolling around, wasting afternoons, and telling a friend.
- **The Sinking Lure.** Invisible, found by rolling through grass against the wall or near the rocks/log, in one of four random spots, only after you'd already earned the pond's prize. The sign says *No Sinking Lures*. Catches with it count for nothing and are labelled on the record wall as taken with an illegal lure. It is a rule you break because you want the fish, not the reward.
- **The Hylian Loach.** Appears by the lily pads on roughly every fourth paid visit. Lies on the bottom; surfaces briefly every few minutes with a *"thap"*, and that is the only moment you can target it. You drop the sinking lure in front of it and **wait one to two minutes without moving**. Disturb it or lose it and it's about 15 minutes before it comes back. Reward: 50 Rupees and it is released as endangered. The payoff is prestige, not loot.
- **The record wall.** Your best fish, weight and name, in a tank by the door. Persisting prestige with no leaderboard.
- **The owner's hat.** As an adult you can hook it off his head. He fines you 50 Rupees. No reward. Pure curiosity bait.
- **Child vs adult.** The same pond, bigger fish seven years later. A reason to come back.
- **It was never planned.** Programmer Morita had a fish model left over from the Water Temple boss and made it swim in a pool *"for when I needed to take a breather."* The rod was a cylinder on Link's sword swing. Aonuma didn't know. The tension music is enemy-combat audio he reused *"to create tension"* without asking. Iwata: *"so complete that it's practically a whole fishing video game"* and it *"doesn't have anything to do with Link's adventure."*

### What people say, 25 years on

- *"It took me and my brother days to catch it."*
- *"I'm still not sure I ever even glimpsed it."* (calls the loach his *white whale*)
- *"after MANY hours and MANY unsuccessful attempts, I managed to reel in the slippery bastard … It was an incredibly amazing feeling to have finally caught it."*
- *"I think I hooked it more than once but lost it each time."*
- *"Spent many hours fishing in that game, analyzing how the pond worked throughout the day."*
- *"He spent long nights in my flat on that pond in search of the fabled Hylian Loach."*
- *"I had this immense sense of satisfaction that lasted for about 5 minutes."*
- *"the one place I really wanted to be whenever I turned the game on."*
- *"the mysterious elongated silhouette who's definitely always sometimes there."*
- *"a pain unlike any other … It's a genuine labor of love."*

Note what is **absent** from the praise: graphics, fish count, upgrades, variety. Every quote is about *time*, *not knowing*, *losing it*, and *finally*.

### It's coming back

The Switch 2 remake of Ocarina of Time ships **5 November 2026** (announced 9 Jun, dated at the Zelda 40th Direct on 8 Sep). Whether the pond is in it or changed: **no source says (unverified)**. The N64 original is on Switch Online. Timing matters: a wave of people are about to re-feel this exact loop in two weeks.

## 2. Why it worked — the five words, as mechanics

| Owner's word | What OoT did | Reel Mobile today | Rebuild must |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Nothing explained. The sinking lure, the loach, the visit counter, the thap sound, sunrise, the log: all found, none told. | No tutorial-pops (good). Two diegetic first-session hints. Species unnamed until landed. | Add **things to find that are not fish**: a lure you earn by looking, a spot that only works at one time, a sign that forbids what works. |
| **The hunt** | Fish are agents: they dart, ignore, follow, spook, flee to the deep. You read shadows for size and stalk the big ones at the log and reeds. | Distance-as-mechanic, fish roam the whole expanse, cues hide identity far out, nearest-fish-catchable. Strong. | Give fish **places and habits**: a big one that lives by the snag, that only rises at dawn, that spooks to the far dark and stays there 15 minutes. |
| **Trial and error** | Tension by feel; too much and it escapes. Sinking lure has to sit still for two minutes. The wrong move costs you the fish, not a life. | Tension band with gold sweet spot, snap jolt, failure aesthetics doc (`11`). Good. | Make **waiting** a skill with a tell (the thap). Make a **wrong cast** cost something small and visible (spooked, gone deep). |
| **The unknown** | Water you cannot see into. *"Definitely always sometimes there."* Even the rule "sinking lure required" turned out not to be airtight. | The Reveal v2: size, shape, cue all hidden in the dark and resolve on the way in. This is the best thing in the game. | Protect it. Then add **one legend**: a fish that is rumoured, rarely seen, and never shown on a card until caught. |
| **Persistence** | Days. 15-minute penalty. Every fourth visit. The record wall with your name. 50 Rupees and released. | Journal, personal-best recognition, share card. One pond, one keeper (spirit of). | Add **visit rhythm** (not daily-login: visit *count*), a **wall** not a list, and a prize whose value is only that you did it. |

### The principles, stated once

1. **Fish are agents, not loot slots.** Behaviour you can learn is the whole hunt.
2. **Hide information, don't tutorialise it.** The pond is a puzzle box with no instructions.
3. **One apex target, rumour-grade.** Rare, conditional, penalised on failure, prestige-only reward.
4. **Observation pays.** Time of day, cover, shadows, sound.
5. **Tactile, readable input.** Stick = lure. Rumble = bite. Tension by hand.
6. **Safe bounded space, zero outside pressure.** *"Nothing happens here unless I want it to."*
7. **Cheap entry, one keeper.** No inventory. Just beat your last fish.
8. **Transgression as discovery.** The banned lure. The hat.
9. **A reward ladder whose last rung is unprized.** Heart → Scale → Loach (50 Rupees, released).
10. **Prestige persistence.** Your name on the wall, not a leaderboard.
11. **Asymmetry over time.** The same pond, different later. A reason to return.
12. **Atmosphere over spectacle.** Running water, shifting light, borrowed tension music.

### What the later games lost (so we don't)

- **Twilight Princess** added a journal, free bobber fishing anywhere, motion controls, seasons. Players note you *can't judge fish size until you catch them*: the readable water was lost. More freedom, less pond.
- **Majora's Mask 3D** added two holes, a collection frame, posters that *explain* the fish. The secret-hunt feel went with the explanation.
- **Stardew Valley** is the all-or-nothing tension bar: *"either relatively easy or impossible, there's almost no middle ground"*; *"the worst part of fishing is the beginning."* Mods exist to delete the minigame. Rare catches still feel *"extra rewarding."*
- **Call of the Wild: The Angler**: ~75% of casts hooked something and a snapped line cost nothing. *"Simplistic and obtuse."* Abundance kills the hunt.
- **Russian Fishing 4**: hourly-bite AFK waits, lure wear, premium grind. Persistence as punishment.
- **Dredge**: dread comes from the *world* (dusk, fog, sanity), the minigame is deliberately simple. Right split.
- **Animal Crossing**: shadow size + shape narrows species without naming it; size ≠ value. Partial reveal, never a blank.
- **Webfishing** (97% positive): secrets *"never clearly signposted"*, so finding one becomes a group moment. Rumour needs an audience.
- **About Fishing** (2026, demo out): *"some things you reel in were never meant to be found."* Nearest competitor to our mystery angle. Play the demo before Chapter 10.

The pattern: every sequel added *more* and lost the pond. **The rebuild adds depth to one body of water, not breadth.**

## 3. The rebuild: "a more realistic 3D immersive game"

### The decision to make first

Realism is spent on **the water, the light and the far shore**, not on free movement. OoT's pond had a fixed-ish third-person camera on a tiny island and nobody remembers it as cramped. Reel's doctrine (`19_THE_FAR_WATER`): you are on the bank, rod in hand, bottom-right. Keep that. What changes is that the bank, the water and the far shore become **real places built from the six reference ponds**, with the kit's water, foliage and light under them.

What "immersive" buys on a phone, in order of return per frame (Birb's hardest-won lesson: *"the largest visual change of the entire previous session was one enum — tone mapping — and every geometry and overlay effort in that session failed"*):

1. **Tone mapping and one colour pipeline.** `NeutralToneMapping` ~1.12 exposure + sRGB. R3F defaults to ACES and nobody has evaluated it on Reel's dusk palette. One line. Do it first and re-grade the palette in `08_ART_DIRECTION` against the reference photos.
2. **Water that reads as water.** This is the biggest gap and the biggest win. Reel's `PondWater` (`Environment.tsx:12-60`) has caustics, no Fresnel, no horizon reflection. Lift from the kit:
   - `waveGradient` analytic normals (3 cosine waves, no texture) and 4-octave `fbm` mottling — `map-assets/water.js:4-8, 21`.
   - Schlick Fresnel `.025 + .78·(1−n·v)^5`, Blinn glint, environment reflection — `water.js:32`.
   - A canvas-gradient equirect environment (zenith → fog colour at the horizon → dark far bank) — `world-shell.js:6`; Birb's water reflects exactly this gradient (`visual-style.js:715-733`).
   - **The dark far water falls out for free**: from a low bank camera the far surface is at grazing angle, Fresnel → .8, and it shows the dark treeline sky. Add one explicit camera-distance darkening term so the gamble zone is tunable, not accidental.
   - **What the kit cannot do and we must add**: the near water is *clear*. The kit's water is opaque. Options, cheapest first: (a) pass fish positions as uniforms and draw them as soft dark blobs *inside* the water shader, alpha by depth and distance — one pass, no transparency sorting, and it *is* the reveal gradient; (b) a transparent surface (`depthWrite:false`) over fish meshes with alpha ≈ 1 − clarity(distance). Not (c): a refraction render target.
   - Glint as `pow(dot(reflect,sun),96)` plus a broad `pow(…,12)` sheen so the highlight has a body and doesn't strobe (Birb).
3. **The far shore.** Instanced fir bough cards (`foliage.js`, ~120 tris/tree, alphaToCoverage needles, the *light-through-needles* hack) sharing one `instanceMatrix` — `slice.js:142-158`. A layered dark treeline frames the pond and makes the far water dark for the right reason (it reflects something dark). Drop to cores on Economy.
4. **The bank in the foreground.** The grass-tuft sway patch — `landscape-details.js:21-28`: one uniform, phase from instance position, amplitude by height — becomes **reeds and cattails** (photo 06). The kit has none; this is the template. Rounded stone bank (photos 01, 05) from `builder-utils.js` batches with `slabTexture()`-style canvas rock. Contact-decal quads under reeds and rocks (Birb `contact-decals.js`) for grounding without shadow maps.
5. **Ambient life, one draw call each.** Stateless GPU points `fract(seed + t·speed)` — `flow-effects.js:4-16`: midges over the water at dusk, bubbles that give a fish away, the hook-set splash. Drifting mist sprites (`slice.js:162-165`) over the far water at dawn, capped ~10, hide the world edge (photo 05).
6. **Wake rings for lure and fish.** One InstancedMesh, round-robin pool, no depth write (Birb `src/effects/wake.js`). Reel's `WaterRipples` sweeps and GCs; switch to the fixed pool.
7. **A slow light.** Birb's `sun-cycle.js` drift that never goes below readable, with glint/sky/mist following for free. This is OoT's sunrise frenzy made visible: the pond is *different at the start of a session than at the end*, and that difference is a tell.
8. **Rim light on the hero object** (fish near the surface, the lure) via Fresnel in `onBeforeCompile` (Birb `visual-style.js:740-760`), so the reveal separates from dark water.
9. **One merged half-res post pass** (bloom on moon and glints + vignette), never EffectComposer (Birb `bloom-pass.js`). Only on the top tier. Optional: GPU Gems radial light shafts through the treeline at dawn.
10. **Short-range `FogExp2`.** Denser fog adds no shader ops. It is the cheapest "far water went dark."

**Do not** embed the `/worlds` viewer. It is a second WebGL context and a second copy of three beside R3F (iOS memory), it carries the turntable/plinth/toolbar, has no pond world and no game hooks, and the energy world is 328 draws / 734k tris: the opposite of a slow pond. **Lift functions and GLSL into R3F components.** The kit's modules are 20–110 lines each; this is a transplant, not a dependency.

**Skip on phones** (both codebases agree, measured): planar Reflector (the kit disables it on mobile), SSAO/SSR/DoF/volumetrics, multi-pass composers, VSM shadows (PCF if any), 120 Hz targets, instance sectors.

### Camera: keep the composed view, borrow the kit's *shots*

The flythrough kernel (`flythrough.js`, 114 lines, no DOM) with `auditShot` (clearance over terrain and water, speed, NaNs) and the actor contract (`activity.js` `stateAt(t)`) give three things cheaply: a seekable **arrival shot** down to the bank at session start; a **reveal push** when a hooked fish crosses into the clear water, synced to the fish's own phase; and deterministic, frame-exact recordings for the share reel (Birb's `frame-clock.js` virtual clock + WebCodecs MP4 is the recording path). Fish schedules as pure functions of scene time mean *the big one rises at the same minute every visit*: that is a discoverable rule.

## 4. Mobile architecture: adopt Birb's discipline, not its code

Birb is 17k lines of `index.html`; Reel's R3F + Zustand + pure `src/game/` split is cleaner. Take the policies.

### Fix now (verified in Reel's source)

- **Boot DPR is stuck at 1.** `GameClient.tsx:62` `useState(1)` feeds `<Canvas dpr>` (`:864`); `runtime.ts:77` separately believes `min(devicePixelRatio, cap)`. Nothing sets the React state until a degrade, which then moves 2 → 1.5 and *raises* resolution on a struggling phone. Birb on exactly this: *"every edge in the world is visibly stepped — it is the single largest thing standing between this and 'sharp'."*
- **Recovery can never fire.** `runtime.ts:325` `fps = 1/max(dt, 1/55)` caps measured fps at 55; `:347` requires `> 55`. A degraded phone stays degraded forever.
- `preserveDrawingBuffer: true` (`GameClient.tsx:865`) costs bandwidth on tile-based iOS GPUs; the catch card draws its own canvas, so it seems only the e2e `toDataURL` check wants it.
- The FPS sampler allocates (`filter`/`map`) every frame (`runtime.ts:328-331`).
- `maxFrameDtSeconds: 0.1` (`tuning.ts:454`); Birb clamps at 0.05. Halve it: bite windows and snap are dt-sensitive.
- `haptics.ts:18` returns when `navigator.vibrate` is missing, so **iPhone has no haptics**. Birb's trick: a hidden `<input type=checkbox switch>` whose `.click()` fires a native tick on iOS 18+ (single ticks only since 26.5). The bite *is* the rumble in OoT. This is not optional.
- `procedural.ts` never sets `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'`: with the ring switch on silent, Reel plays nothing. One line, feature-detected.

### Adopt (S effort each unless marked)

- **One sizing function** for resize, orientation, tier change and context restore (Birb `updateRendererSize`), rAF-coalesced. Reel's context-loss handler only flips flags.
- **Pause the loop** behind splash, result card, gear select (`frameloop="demand"` or explicit). A pond is mostly still; this is battery and heat.
- **Shed order doctrine:** effects → distant detail → resolution. DPR alone cannot fix a fill-bound frame. The kit's `adaptive-quality.js` (32 lines, pure, 5 tiers, p90 windows, 4 s warm-up, hysteresis, pixel budget ~1.1 MP mobile) drops straight into `useFrame`.
- **Player-facing Auto chip** (`perf-advisor.js`, pure, tested): offers a step down, never forces it. Yellow < 48 fps 3 s, red < 38, clears at 54 for 6 s, 4 s grace after boot/preset/resume.
- **Reset FPS history** on resume, restore, orientation, or the first frame back reads as 5 fps and trips the tier.
- **Audio:** gesture-scoped re-unlock on every touchstart (iOS kills the context on a call or Siri and only resumes inside a gesture); `userActivation.isActive` check; fade-then-suspend; idle watchdog on a frame counter.
- **PWA:** PNG `apple-touch-icon` and maskable PNGs (Reel is SVG-only; iOS ignores SVG icons). Defer the SW update reload mid-fight (Birb `__birbMiniGameActive`).
- **Touch:** radial deadzone, not per-axis; diagonal normalisation; release decay instead of snap-to-zero. Spring-damper (C0 8, C1 6, `aim-rig.js`) for a rod tip with weight.

### Validation doctrine (the thought leadership, verbatim where it counts)

- *"An assertion nobody has watched fail is not an oracle, it is a hope."* Every gate in `12_VALIDATION` gets a planted defect that must fail it.
- *"An agent that writes both the code and its test is a closed loop that proves nothing."* Oracle hash manifest; no agent edits its own oracle.
- *"`effective` is READ BACK, never echoed."* A headless check that reads the renderer's actual DPR on an emulated 3× iPhone would have caught both DPR bugs above. Add it to CI first.
- **Fail on console warnings**, not just errors, with `if: !cancelled()` so one red step doesn't hide the rest.
- *"No SwiftShader number ever becomes a device claim."* The iPhone gate (`16_HUMAN_GATES`) stays the only feel authority. 10+ minutes on glass for thermals.
- **Capture sidecars** `{seed, time, tier, build, pose}`: *"A capture without a sidecar is not evidence."* And freeze the light for A/Bs: *"an A/B taken three minutes apart measures the time of day."*
- **Blind paired forced-choice** against a frozen Shipping Reference at every art stage, win threshold stated in advance. *"'The owner scored at chance' has to be a legitimate, recordable, non-shameful stage outcome."*
- **Commit the target first:** reference images (the six ponds) plus a 20–30 item binary rubric *before* building the water.
- **Spend on light, not polygons. Debug by turning one thing off. Deletion often beats optimisation.** *"A control that lies is worse than no control."*
- **Owner attention is the scarce resource.** Batch the gates; never ship a variant sheet with one option.

## 5. New mechanics the rebuild should add (and the doors that stay shut)

Each is one of the five words made concrete. None is in `14_DO_NOT_BUILD`; each is checked against it.

1. **The legend.** One fish. Lives in the far dark by a specific feature (the lily pads, photo 04). Surfaces briefly every N minutes with its own sound — the only moment it can be targeted. Takes only a *still* lure, held for a long count. Lost or spooked: gone for the session's equivalent of 15 minutes. Appears only on some visits (visit count, not clock). Reward: a line on the wall and release. No card, no stars. *(Not a "boss fish encounter mechanic": no HP, no phases, no cinematic. It is a shy fish with a schedule.)*
2. **The banned lure.** A fourth lure that is not in the strip. Found, not bought: it is *in the world* (snagged on the far snag, photo 06; under the bank). The pond's sign forbids it. Catches with it are marked on the wall as such. This is `22_THE_GEAR`'s "never a 4th lure" rule bent on purpose: it is not a sidegrade in the picker, it is a secret with a cost. **Owner decision required.**
3. **Time that moves.** One slow light cycle per session (dawn → day, or dusk → night, chosen per visit), with a feeding window at the turn. Not a weather system, not a day/night *cycle* in the DO-NOT sense (no clock UI, no time travel): one gradient that moves and that fish respond to. **Owner decision required** against `14`'s "one fixed twilight mood for MVP."
4. **Places.** Three or four named features on one pond (the log, the reeds, the stone pool, the far snag). Fish have home ranges. The big ones hold cover. Learnable, never labelled.
5. **The wall.** Replace the journal's list-feel with the record tank: your best, your name, its lure. One keeper.
6. **The spook rule.** A bad cast near a fish sends it to the far dark for a while. Trial and error with a visible, non-punitive cost.
7. **The hat.** One piece of transgressive curiosity bait that rewards nothing. (What it is: TBD. Something on the bank you can hook.)

**Stays shut:** multiple ponds, economy, XP, rarity stars, daily login, leaderboards, NPC dialogue, quests, boats, weather system, aquarium. The sequels proved that *more* is how you lose the pond.

## 6. Proposed order

1. **Pipeline fixes** (DPR boot/recover, dt clamp, haptics, audioSession, effective-state CI check). One PR, measurable on the phone the same day.
2. **Light and water** (tone mapping, Fresnel/gradient water with in-shader fish blobs, FogExp2, glint). Rubric and reference images committed first; blind A/B against today's build.
3. **Shore and bank** (fir treeline, reeds, stone bank, contact decals, mist). Budget: hold the kit's and Birb's numbers: < 100 draws, aim < 50k tris, ~1.1 MP.
4. **Life** (wake pool, GPU midges/bubbles, slow light, fish home ranges + spook).
5. **The legend and the wall.**
6. **The banned lure and the hat** (after the owner's call).
7. **Arrival shot and reveal push** via the flythrough kernel; studio recording for the alpha site.

Each step ends at the iPhone gate. No step ships on a SwiftShader number.
